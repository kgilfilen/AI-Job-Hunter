# Stuff to remember when using AI to develop

## Seeing the trees in the forest

With AI writing code and tests, and everything, you start to only see the forest, and lose track of the code you are writing, you have to still have a feel for what you are writing.

### Have a day of using the software

You might forget what you are developing, and why.

### Go from being managed, to 25% self-management

Because you can do so much so quickly, it is easy to drift all over a large ocean of possibilities. In a managed environment, your manager might check on you once a day at scrum. You need to stay basically on the tasks for the day.

## You are the architect, not AI

Sure, AI is writing a lot of the code, maybe all of it. You are still the one who is responsible for it. If your new website gets hit all the time, due to great advertising, but NOBODY converts to a sale, it is not AI's fault. You are the architect.

## A day down the rabbit hole can produce a ton of crap

You can easily go wherever it feels interesting, and create something unnecessary, full of cruft, and ultimately trash. Maybe productivity is not so important now? Maybe it is. 

## Stuff you should do

### Define milestones somehow, and with details. AI can help dramatically with that. Then you just ask AI if you have completed a milestone, Sprint 2 for example, and then do the transition, completing all of the Agile artifacts. AI can help with that too.

Because when you are deep in the forest you might forget what the whole project is for, or you might forget what this feature solves.

### Make sure AI is working from the real code, not guessing what you both wrote two days ago. Give AI the current code when making modifications, and request the full file back for copy and paste to Visual Code.

It is very possible that AI creates a generic file with some incorrect imports, some incorrect function names, etc. If you get a complete file back, you reduce the chances of creating difficult errors. 

### Correct AI to your preferred style of code and comments. 

I like to write "Loops through ALL employees", not "Loop through ALL employees"; I want to say what it does, not what I want it to do.

### Recognize that AI is constantly encouraging you, not that it loves you and your dog. 

AI is a "mindless" but brilliant computer sitting and waiting for its next task. It is not in a conversation with you like a drinking buddy. When it is not answering a question, it is asleep, and uncaring. This is probably more for your kid brother, who is using AI like a personal therapist. We need to remember AI looks great, like a well-trained therapist who is snoring behind his tablet. 

### Encourage AI to develop good tests as you go; unit tests and integration tests. 

The time WILL come when you make significant changes to a medium sized code base that damage things. If you don't have tests, you may not know what got broken. Other issues might go undetected, you just notice that fit scoring seems optimistic. Good unit tests catch things in cascades, you fix one thing and it points to another issue.
